# Research 1: Benchmarking portfolios for Shimaa Essam (content creator, EN/AR)

Date: 2026-10-16. Method: WebSearch + WebFetch of live sites (text extraction, so motion/visual notes are limited to what was described in markup or listing pages). Some sites blocked fetching (jacobmcmillen.com 403, rachelkarten.com empty, emanismail.com empty, mycodelesswebsite.com bot wall, webflow blog header overflow) and are excluded.

Caveat: Several short-form editor sites below are Framer templates/demos with placeholder data (Jumpcut, Reelhaus, Editor). They are included because they encode what the short-form editing market converges on (layout, proof, pricing), not as evidence of real results.

## A. Individual sites studied

### 1. K.J. Brett (freelance copywriter, UK) - https://kjbrett.com/
- Hero: no headline formula; one-line intro "Kim is an award-winning freelance copywriter based in Hertfordshire, UK." (role + credential + location).
- Order: Intro -> Portfolio -> Brands -> Kind Words -> Accolades -> Contact.
- Work: only 2 tiles. "Full portfolio" (PDF) with subtitle listing deliverable types ("Brand messaging | 360-degree campaigns | Email copy | Landing pages..."), and "Long-form copy" page. CTA "Click to view".
- Proof: brand names as text (Hotel Chocolat, Neal's Yard Remedies, Anthropologie, Chupi), 4 testimonials each with name + role + company (client-side copy managers, i.e. people who hire), 3 accolades with year.
- Contact: availability line "Currently open to new projects" + plain email. Zero friction, no form.
- Tone: warm, understated. One page, pared back. Authory highlights the color + symmetry as strengths.
- Takeaway: one-pager can win with brands + attributed testimonials + availability signal. Weakness: work hidden behind a PDF.

### 2. Shanley Cox (copywriter / verbal strategist) - https://www.shanleycox.com/
- Hero: "Hi, I'm Shanley Cox - copywriter and verbal strategist." then "see what I can do. ☞" (pointer into work).
- Work: stacked project cards: mockup image, pipe-separated service tags ("verbal strategy | brand messaging | web copy"), project name, "See the Work". 6 projects.
- Proof: the work + client names only. No testimonials/metrics.
- Contact: closing "Let's get to work" + mailto.
- Takeaway: Hero -> work immediately; service tags on each card tell the client what she did on that project. Good model for Shimaa's mixed-skill projects (script + edit + design on one client).

### 3. Evan Benner (copywriter, Chicago) - https://evanbenner.com/
- Hero: portrait + "Welcome to the copywriting portfolio and author homepage of Evan Benner, beaming to you from Chicago."
- Work: uniform grid of thumbnails, titles in "Client - Deliverable" pattern ("Hormel Chili - Social Recipe Videos"), year. 27 items, 2011-2024. No descriptions on grid.
- Proof: big brand names in titles (Hormel, Ballast Point, Marriott, SKIPPY, NASA).
- Contact: separate page (extra click), unlabeled social icons.
- Takeaway: "Client - Deliverable" naming is instantly scannable. Avoid: 27 items with no curation, duplicate nav blocks, no on-page contact, Adobe Portfolio generic feel.

### 4. Bigmouth Copy (Taylor de la Fuente, agency) - https://bigmouthcopy.com/
- Hero: "The room already knows you." / "Now the internet will too." Sub: "Copywriting & Design for Thought Leaders Whose Website Hasn't Caught Up with Their Real-World Reputation." CTA "Fix My Website" (outcome-phrased, first-person).
- Nav: Services, Portfolio, About, Book Us.
- Order: Hero -> what you get (5 bullets incl. "4-week delivery") -> founder bio + 1 testimonial -> values -> "Big Mouth, Bigger Results" + "Let's Talk".
- Proof: one celebrity-ish testimonial (Amy Landino), specifics like 4-week delivery, referral 10%.
- Tone: bold, irreverent; a clear niche identity (bubblegum-punk visual per Authory).
- Takeaway: headline names the buyer and their pain; CTA verb is the outcome. Weakness: homepage shows no actual work.

### 5. Punchline Copy (Lianna Patch, conversion copy for SaaS) - https://punchlinecopy.com/
- Hero: eyebrow "Killer copy in 4 weeks"; H1 "Let's fix your blah, boring, 'I'd rather die than read this' copy"; sub names audience (SaaS teams) and metrics (MRR, ARPU, LTV); CTA "Book a call" (Calendly).

- Proof: 7 logos high on page, metric testimonial ("unsubscribe rate dropped 50%"), screenshots of founders praising her.
- Friction: Calendly only, price only in FAQ.
- Takeaway: the site *is* the writing sample - every line demonstrates the voice she sells. Testimonials interleaved between sections instead of one block. Productized named offer.

### 6. Kaleigh Moore (SaaS content / AI search) - https://www.kaleighmoore.com/

## B. Short-form / vertical video market patterns (templates + demos)

### 14. Picturelock (Framer template for freelance editors) - https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/picturelock/

### 19. Market data on vertical / UGC portfolios
- JoinBrands (https://joinbrands.com/blog/ugc-portfolio-examples/): 8-12 best videos, not 20+; group by category; one line per video ("Skincare product launch - 60-second hook-focused ad"); for storytelling add ask/approach/result; brands should decide within ~2 minutes; show range of hooks (question, surprising statement, relatable struggle); rates, availability, turnaround must be visible.
- UGC guide summary (search result, spotlightwp / rondocin): hero with best video, grid of 3-6 standouts, logos, short "how I work", testimonial, one CTA.

- Fueler scriptwriting (https://fueler.io/blog/strong-youtube-scriptwriting-portfolio-projects): package full concept (title, hook, structure), "rewrite a weak script" sample showing before/after hook.
- Upwork market (Sept 2026 job search snippet): small businesses hire 9:16 editors with trial weeks and ask specifically for TikTok/Reels-style samples.

### 20. Reviewer behavior
- uxdesign.cc "Only 30 seconds to reject your portfolio" (https://uxdesign.cc/only-30-seconds-to-reject-your-portfolio-8cb14ac70674): ~2s to decide to look further, ~30s more to judge; many reviewers on phones between meetings. Unverified, practitioner sources, but consistent: the first screen must sell.

## C. Cross-cutting patterns (what the best do)

1. **Hero = who you help + outcome + one primary CTA, all above the fold.** Bigmouth ("Copywriting & Design for Thought Leaders whose website hasn't caught up..."), Punchline (audience + metrics in sub), Kaleigh Moore (problem-statement H1). Weaker sites (Evan Benner, Jackie Letkowski, Lauren Hom) open with "Hi, I'm X" and no buyer.
   - For Shimaa: e.g. "Short-form scripts and reels that make people stop scrolling - for education and lifestyle brands." Primary CTA: "Start a project" / WhatsApp; secondary: "Watch the reels".
2. **Proof directly under the hero.** Logo/brand strip immediately below hero: Kaleigh Moore, Sam Kolder, Punchline. With 2 clients, show both client names/logos ("Content for: [Language institute] · [Brand]") plus a work-volume stat (e.g. "14 reels · 12 designs · 3 content plans") instead of fake big numbers.
3. **Show work within one scroll.** Shanley Cox ("see what I can do ☞" straight into projects), Sam Kolder (logos then grid). Sites that hide work behind PDFs or nav (K.J. Brett PDF, Bigmouth, Lauren Hom) lose the 30-second scan.
4. **Curate hard: 4-6 featured, rest behind "See all".** Sam Kolder 4, Jumpcut 4 + See all, JoinBrands 8-12, Fueler 6-10. Evan Benner's 27 uncurated tiles is the anti-pattern. Shimaa: feature 4-6 best reels on home, full set of 14 on Work.
5. **Vertical video in true 9:16 frames, grouped by format, lightweight playback.** Picturelock (real aspect ratio, load on play), Editor template (vertical rail with view-count overlay), Sam Kolder (looping GIF preview). Pattern: muted looping poster/preview in a 9:16 card, tap to open a player with sound; 2 columns on mobile, 3-5 on desktop or a horizontal snap rail. Never letterbox 9:16 inside 16:9.
6. **Every work item has a one-line context label + role tags.** "Client - Deliverable" (Evan Benner), service tags (Shanley Cox), "Skincare product launch - 60-second hook-focused ad" (JoinBrands), role/deliverables/brief/result (Picturelock). Shimaa: "Language institute - Spanish course promo reel · Script + Edit", with EN/AR.
7. **Case studies = brief -> approach -> result, short.** Picturelock project page; JoinBrands "Storyteller" format; Editor template metric triplet. For her 3 buyer-persona PDFs: make one "Content planning" case study (client, goal, persona research, the plan, sample outputs that came from it) with the PDF as a download, not as the only view.
8. **Numbers when real, attributed when possible.** Paddy (700m views/month; testimonial givers with subscriber counts), Punchline (unsubscribe down 50%), Reelhaus (avg watch time). If Shimaa has any reel views/engagement or enrollment uplift from the institute, put it on the card. Don't invent stats (templates show inflated, inconsistent placeholders - Editor demo has "40+ clients" vs "100+ creators").
9. **Testimonials with full attribution, interleaved.** K.J. Brett (name + role + company), Punchline (testimonials placed between sections, plus screenshot wall). One quote from the institute's marketing lead and one from the brand owner, with role, beats any number of anonymous ones. Screenshot of a WhatsApp/Instagram praise message is a valid, culturally native format.
10. **Low-friction contact repeated 3+ times, with availability + response time.** K.J. Brett "Currently open to new projects" + email; Kaleigh Moore "call within 2 business days"; Jumpcut "response within 24 hours"; Editor "Available for work" badge; Vimeo tip "several CTAs to contact form". In Egypt/MENA, WhatsApp is the lowest-friction channel - add it alongside email and a short form. Picturelock-style short form (service, deadline, budget range) qualifies leads.
11. **Make services + process + starting price visible (productize).** Punchline "4-Week Facelift", Kaleigh's badged offers ("Start Here"), Jumpcut process (5 steps) and per-video price, Paddy's self-segmenting tiers. Shimaa: 3 offers (e.g. "Reel package: script + edit", "Monthly content plan + calendar", "Social design set"), each with deliverables, turnaround, and "from" price or "request a quote".
12. **Dual audience paths: hire-me vs. recruit-me.** Most freelancer sites ignore employers; since she wants job offers too, a "Download CV" secondary CTA in nav/about + an About with skills/tools (Jumpcut "Tools I use": CapCut, Premiere, Canva, etc.) serves recruiters without cluttering the client path.
13. **Voice is the proof for a writer.** Punchline, Talking Shrimp, Bigmouth - copy itself demonstrates skill (hooks, microcopy like "me want", "Fix My Website"). For a scriptwriter, headings written as hooks and CTA labels with personality show the craft; in Arabic, use natural Egyptian/MSA register deliberately (not machine translation).
14. **Distinct visual identity beats generic minimal.** Bigmouth (bubblegum punk), Social Media Manager template ("make it a personality", scrapbook), Shanley's playful pointer. But work must remain the visual hero - restrained chrome around colorful work.
15. **Restrained motion.** Marquee strips (Kaleigh Moore, SMM template), looping previews (Sam Kolder), scroll-format morph (Picturelock). Motion supports scanning; nothing blocks content. Load video on interaction for performance.

## D. What to avoid
- Homepage with no work (Bigmouth, Talking Shrimp, Jackie Letkowski, Gio Marcus) - only works with big reputations.
- Work only as a PDF download (K.J. Brett) - PDFs are a backup, not the display.
- Unlabeled thumbnails/grids with no client or role (Lauren Hom, Evan Benner).
- Contact on a separate page only or hidden (Evan Benner, Lauren Hom, Nomad).
- Fake urgency popups (Paddy Galloway), inflated/inconsistent stats (Editor demo), placeholder content left in (Jumpcut FAQ mismatch, typos in Reelhaus "directors's").
- Too many items uncurated (27 at Evan Benner); off-niche content (JoinBrands: exclude unrelated personal videos).
- Autoplaying 14 videos with sound / heavy embeds (Picturelock loads on play).
- Calendly-only contact with price buried (Punchline) is fine for $15K SaaS, wrong for MENA small-business clients who expect WhatsApp.

## E. Concrete page plan suggestion (for ~14 reels, ~12 designs, 3 persona PDFs, CV)
1. Hero: outcome headline + audience, sub-line listing the 4 skills, "Available for projects" badge, primary CTA (WhatsApp/Start a project), secondary (Watch reels). Optional muted looping 9:16 reel in a phone frame beside the text (RTL-mirrored in Arabic).
2. Client strip: 2 client logos/names + work-volume facts.
3. Featured reels: 4-6 9:16 cards, muted loop/poster, label "Client - Format · Role", tap to open player; "See all 14 reels".
4. Social design: masonry/grid of 6, lightbox, "See all 12".
5. Content planning case study: 1 card leading into persona research -> plan -> output; PDF downloads.
6. Services (3 offers) + 4-step process + turnaround.
7. Testimonials (2 attributed, maybe a screenshot).
8. About + tools + Download CV.
9. Closing CTA: WhatsApp, email, short form (service, deadline, budget), response-time promise.
